# Evidence-Based Programming Review: Literature vs. Lift Coach App

## Purpose
Systematic review of peer-reviewed and published sources on powerlifting programming, strength training methodology, and evidence-based lifting practice. Each source is documented with citation, key findings, and a comparison to what our Lift Coach app currently implements.

---

## Source 1: Schoenfeld et al. — Dose-Response Volume and Hypertrophy

**Citation:** Schoenfeld, B.J., Ogborn, D., & Krieger, J.W. (2017). Dose-response relationship between weekly resistance training volume and increases in muscle mass: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Journal of Sports Sciences*, 35(11), 1073-1082. PMID: 27433992.

**Key Findings:**
- Meta-analysis of 34 treatment groups from 15 studies
- Each additional weekly set associated with ES increase of 0.023 (0.37% greater hypertrophy)
- Higher vs lower volume groups showed ES difference of 0.241 (~3.9% greater gains)
- Three volume tiers: <5 sets, 5-9 sets, 10+ sets per muscle per week showed dose-response trend (P = 0.074)
- "Graded dose-response relationship whereby increases in RT volume produce greater gains in muscle hypertrophy"

**Programming Recommendations:**
- Minimum: 5+ sets per muscle group per week for meaningful hypertrophy
- Better: 10+ sets per muscle group per week
- Volume should be progressively increased over mesocycles
- No upper ceiling identified in this study (later work suggests 20+ may have diminishing returns)

**App Comparison:**
- Our app prescribes **4 sets for hypertrophy phase** — this is at the LOW end of evidence
- No guidance on weekly volume across sessions (total sets per muscle group per week)
- No concept of volume progression within a mesocycle (start low, build up)
- **GAP: App should recommend 10-20 sets/muscle/week distributed across 2-3 sessions, not just 4 sets per workout**

## Source 2: Schoenfeld et al. — Low vs High Load Meta-Analysis

**Citation:** Schoenfeld, B.J., Grgic, J., Ogborn, D., & Krieger, J.W. (2017). Strength and hypertrophy adaptations between low- vs. high-load resistance training: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 31(12), 3508-3523. PMID: 28834797.

**Key Findings:**
- Compared low-load (≤60% 1RM) vs high-load (>60% 1RM) training, all sets to failure
- 21 studies included
- **1RM strength gains significantly greater with high loads** (>60% 1RM)
- **Muscle hypertrophy was SIMILAR between low and high loads** when trained to failure
- Isometric strength showed no difference between conditions

**Programming Recommendations:**
- For maximal strength (powerlifting): Must use heavy loads (>60% 1RM, ideally 80%+)
- For hypertrophy: Load range is flexible (even 30-60% 1RM works if taken close to failure)
- Accessory work can use lighter loads for hypertrophy without compromising muscle growth
- Validates traditional powerlifting periodization: heavy compounds for strength + moderate accessories for size

**App Comparison:**
- Our hypertrophy phase: 65-75% 1RM — this is **correctly within evidence**, though on the conservative side
- Our strength phase: 80-90% 1RM — **correctly aligned with evidence**
- Our peaking phase: 90-100% 1RM — **correctly aligned**
- **GAP: App doesn't differentiate load recommendations between main lifts and accessory work. Accessories could use lighter loads (50-70%) for hypertrophy while main lifts stay heavier**

## Source 3: Schoenfeld et al. — Training Frequency and Hypertrophy

**Citation:** Schoenfeld, B.J., Ogborn, D., & Krieger, J.W. (2016). Effects of resistance training frequency on measures of muscle hypertrophy: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Sports Medicine*, 46(11), 1689-1697. PMID: 27102172.

**Key Findings:**
- Meta-analysis of 10 studies comparing weekly training frequencies
- Training 2x/week produced significantly greater hypertrophy than 1x/week (ES: 0.49 vs 0.30)
- Insufficient data to determine if 3x/week > 2x/week
- "Major muscle groups should be trained at least twice a week to maximize muscle growth"
- When volume is equated, frequency itself may not matter (later 2019 follow-up confirmed this)

**Programming Recommendations:**
- Train each muscle group at minimum 2x per week
- Distribute weekly volume across 2-3 sessions rather than 1 high-volume session
- Frequency can be used as a tool to manage per-session volume (more sessions = less volume per session)

**App Comparison:**
- **Our app has NO frequency recommendation system at all**
- No guidance on how many days per week to train each lift
- No weekly programming structure (just single-session prescriptions)
- **GAP: App needs to recommend training frequency (e.g., squat 2-3x/week, bench 2-3x/week, deadlift 1-2x/week) and weekly split structure**

## Source 4: Zourdos et al. — RIR-Based RPE Scale Validation

**Citation:** Zourdos, M.C., et al. (2016). Novel resistance training-specific rating of perceived exertion scale measuring repetitions in reserve. *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 30(1), 267-275. PMID: 26049792. (Also referenced: Helms et al. 2016, PMC4961270)

**Key Findings:**
- RIR-based RPE scale validated for resistance training autoregulation
- Experienced lifters more accurate than novices at estimating RPE (r = -0.88 vs r = -0.77 for velocity correlation)
- Accuracy improves at higher intensities: SD at 100% = 0.32, at 60% = 1.18
- Novices should practice RPE rating before using it for load prescription

**RPE Targets by Training Goal:**
| Goal | RPE Range | RIR | Notes |
|------|-----------|-----|-------|
| Hypertrophy | 6-8 | 2-4 | Main lifts; avoid excessive fatigue |
| Strength | 8-9 | 1-2 | Include some RPE 10 for specificity |
| Power | ≤4 | High | Maximal intent, maintain speed |

**Programming Recommendations:**
- Combine RPE with percentage-based loading (e.g., "3x3 at 90% 1RM, RPE 8-9")
- Use RPE to autoregulate daily load selection (±5% based on readiness)
- RPE works best as complement to percentage-based loading, not replacement
- Reserve RPE-only programming for experienced lifters

**App Comparison:**
- Our app estimates RPE from angular velocity ratios — this is an **approximate proxy**, not true RPE
- App correctly disclaims this is "NOT equivalent to validated VBT measurements"
- RPE targets per phase: Hypertrophy RPE 7-8, Strength RPE 8-9, Peaking RPE 9-10, Deload RPE 5-6
- **Hypertrophy RPE target is slightly high** vs evidence (should be 6-8, not 7-8)
- **GAP: App should guide users on combining RPE WITH percentage-based loading (dual prescription)**
- **GAP: App should educate users that RPE accuracy requires experience and provide calibration guidance**
- **GAP: No autoregulation protocol — app should suggest "if RPE exceeds target by 1+, reduce load 5%"**

## Source 5: Travis et al. — Tapering and Peaking for Powerlifting

**Citation:** Travis, S.K., Mujika, I., Gentles, J.A., Stone, M.H., & Bazyler, C.D. (2020). Tapering and peaking maximal strength for powerlifting performance: A review. *Sports*, 8(9), 125. PMC7552788.

**Key Findings:**
- Step and exponential tapers most effective for powerlifting
- Optimal volume reduction: 30-70%, with 30-50% appearing most effective
- Optimal taper duration: 1-2 weeks (7-14 days strongest evidence)
- Maintain training intensity ≥85% 1RM during taper (or reduce slightly)
- Training cessation: 2-7 days before competition (2-4 days optimal)
- Expected improvement: 2-10% for individual lifts, 3-4% for total
- Large volume reductions + high intensity = negative outcomes (caution)
- Deadlift may benefit from earlier cessation (1-2 weeks before meet)

**Specific Taper Protocols:**
- Week 1: Reduce volume 30-50%, maintain intensity at ~85-90% 1RM
- Week 2: Further reduce volume, possible slight intensity reduction
- Final 2-4 days: Complete rest or very light movement
- Round to 7-10 day total taper for most lifters

**App Comparison:**
- Our peaking phase prescribes: 4 sets, 1-3 reps, 90-100% 1RM, RPE 9-10
- Notes mention "reduce total volume while maintaining or increasing intensity" — partially aligned
- **But no taper timeline** (how many weeks, when to start before competition)
- **No volume reduction protocol** (specific percentages per week)
- **No training cessation guidance** (when to stop training before meet day)
- **No distinction between early peaking and final taper**
- **GAP: App needs multi-week peaking protocol: e.g., 3 weeks out → 2 weeks out → 1 week out → meet day**
- **GAP: App should differentiate per-lift taper timing (deadlift stops earlier)**
- **GAP: App needs "meet day" planning (attempt selection is there, but prep timeline is missing)**

## Source 6: Helms et al. — RPE for Volume Autoregulation

**Citation:** Helms, E.R., Cross, M.R., Brown, S.R., Storey, A., Cronin, J., & Zourdos, M.C. (2018). Rating of perceived exertion as a method of volume autoregulation within a periodized program. *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*, 32(6), 1627-1636. PMID: 29786623.

**Key Findings:**
- 12 nationally qualified powerlifters tested across hypertrophy, power, and strength phases
- "RPE stops" protocol: perform sets until target RPE is reached, then stop
- Larger load reductions between sets = more total volume (2% drops: 74.6 sets vs 6% drops: 114.4 sets)
- Volume can be effectively autoregulated using RPE stops
- This approach automatically adjusts training quantity based on daily readiness

**Programming Recommendations:**
- Use "RPE stop" method: prescribe target RPE, let volume self-regulate
- On good days, lifters naturally accumulate more volume; on bad days, less
- Back-off sets with 2-6% load reductions after top set
- This replaces fixed set prescriptions (e.g., "do 5 sets" → "do sets until RPE 8")

**App Comparison:**
- Our app uses fixed set prescriptions per phase (hypertrophy: 4 sets, strength: 5 sets, etc.)
- No concept of back-off sets or RPE-stop protocols
- **GAP: App should offer autoregulated volume — "perform sets until RPE reaches X" instead of fixed set counts**
- **GAP: App should include back-off set recommendations (e.g., "after top set at RPE 8, do 2-3 back-off sets at -10% load")**

## Source 7: Helms et al. — RPE vs Percentage-Based Loading

**Citation:** Helms, E.R., Byrnes, R.K., Cooke, D.M., et al. (2018). RPE vs. Percentage 1RM loading in periodized programs matched for sets and repetitions. *Frontiers in Physiology*, 9, 247. PMID: 29628895.

**Key Findings:**
- 8-week study comparing RPE-based vs %-based loading in resistance-trained males
- Daily undulating periodization for squat and bench (3x/week)
- Both approaches produced significant strength gains
- RPE group: +27.75 kg combined 1RM vs %-group: +23.55 kg (not significantly different)
- RPE may provide small advantage (72% probability of small ES superiority)
- "Both loading-types are effective"

**Programming Recommendations:**
- Either RPE or percentage-based loading works for trained lifters
- RPE allows daily adjustment for readiness; percentage provides structure
- Best approach: combine both (e.g., "work up to 85% 1RM, should feel like RPE 8")
- Daily undulating periodization effective for both approaches

**App Comparison:**
- Our app uses percentage-based loading when 1RM is known, RPE when not
- This is an either/or approach rather than combined
- **GAP: App should present DUAL prescriptions: "185-210 lbs (65-75% 1RM) — should feel like RPE 7-8"**
- **GAP: No daily undulating periodization option (app has only linear phase model)**

## Source 8: Helms et al. — Contest Preparation Recommendations

**Citation:** Helms, E.R., Fitschen, P.J., Aragon, A.A., Cronin, J., & Schoenfeld, B.J. (2015). Recommendations for natural bodybuilding contest preparation: resistance and cardiovascular training. *Journal of Sports Medicine and Physical Fitness*, 55(3), 164-178. PMID: 24998610.

**Key Findings:**
- Evidence-based training pyramid hierarchy (most to least important):
  1. **Adherence** (foundation)
  2. **Volume, Intensity, Frequency** (core programming variables)
  3. **Progression** (progressive overload methods)
  4. **Exercise Selection** (compound → isolation hierarchy)
  5. **Rest Periods** (1-3 min standard, longer for heavy compounds)
  6. **Tempo** (1-2s concentric, 2-3s eccentric)

**Specific Recommendations:**
- Volume: 40-70 reps per muscle group per session
- Intensity: Most work at 70-80% 1RM (6-12 rep range)
- Frequency: Each muscle group 2x/week minimum
- Tempo: 1-2s concentric, 2-3s eccentric
- Training to failure: Limit on heavy compounds, reserve for lighter isolation work
- Rest: 1-3 min (longer for heavy compounds)
- Periodization: Block and undulating models both effective

**App Comparison:**
- Our app recommends 2-3s eccentric for hypertrophy — **aligned**
- Rest recommendations present (1-2 min hypertrophy, 3-5 min strength) — **aligned**
- **GAP: No concept of training to failure guidelines (when it's safe vs when to avoid)**
- **GAP: No exercise selection hierarchy (main lift → variation → accessory → isolation)**
- **GAP: No per-session volume target (reps per muscle group per session)**

## Source 9: Renaissance Periodization — Volume Landmarks (Israetel)

**Citation:** Israetel, M., Hoffmann, J., Davis, M., et al. Renaissance Periodization: Training Volume Landmarks for Muscle Growth. RP Strength, 2024. (Published methodology, widely cited)

**Key Findings — Volume Landmarks per Muscle Group (weekly sets):**

| Muscle Group | MV (Maintain) | MEV (Min Growth) | MAV (Optimal) | MRV (Max Recovery) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quads | 6 | 8 | 12-18 | 20 |
| Hamstrings | 4 | 6 | 10-16 | 20 |
| Glutes | 0 | 0 | 4-12 | 16 |
| Chest | 4 | 6 | 12-20 | 22 |
| Back | 8 | 10 | 14-22 | 25 |
| Biceps | 5 | 8 | 14-20 | 26 |
| Triceps | 4 | 6 | 10-14 | 18 |
| Front Delts | 0 | 0 | 6-8 | 12 |
| Side/Rear Delts | 0 | 8 | 16-22 | 26 |

**Mesocycle Volume Progression:**
- Start mesocycle at MEV
- Progress weekly toward MAV
- Never exceed MRV
- Deload after 4-8 weeks back to MEV or below
- RIR progression: Start at 3-4 RIR, end at 0-1 RIR

**App Comparison:**
- Our app has NO per-muscle-group volume guidance
- No concept of MEV/MAV/MRV
- No volume progression within mesocycles
- Deload phase exists but without connection to volume landmarks
- **GAP: App should track weekly volume per muscle group and alert when approaching MRV**
- **GAP: App should implement volume progression within mesocycle (week 1: MEV → week 4-6: near MRV → deload)**
- **GAP: RIR progression within mesocycle is missing (start easy, end hard)**

## Source 10: Greg Nuckols — 28 Free Programs (Stronger by Science)

**Citation:** Nuckols, G. 28 Free Programs. Stronger by Science, 2016-present. (Available at strongerbyscience.com/newsletter)

**Key Findings:**
- 28 program modules covering squat, bench, and deadlift
- Three skill tiers: beginner, intermediate, advanced
- Three frequency options: 1x, 2x, 3x per week per lift
- 4-week cycles with 1RM testing after each cycle
- Percentage-based loading
- Beginners: stick to main lifts (squat/bench/deadlift)
- Intermediate/Advanced: introduce variations (front squat, pause squat, deficit deadlift, etc.)
- Users mix-and-match modules per lift to build complete program

**Programming Recommendations:**
- Beginners: 2x frequency, focus on technique, linear progression
- Intermediate: 2-3x frequency, introduce variations, undulating intensity
- Advanced: 3x frequency, more variations, higher total volume
- Each lift gets its own module — allows individualized programming per lift
- 4-week mesocycle length is standard

**App Comparison:**
- Our app has a single prescription per phase with no frequency guidance
- No concept of exercise variations (pause squat, front squat, tempo deadlift, etc.)
- No mesocycle length guidance
- No program mixing/matching capability
- **GAP: App should recommend exercise variations per phase (hypertrophy: tempo/paused variants, strength: competition lifts)**
- **GAP: App should structure 4-week mesocycles with built-in progression**
- **GAP: App should recommend different frequencies per lift and per experience level**

## Source 11: Nuckols — Optimal Training Volume (Stronger by Science)

**Citation:** Nuckols, G. (2018). The New Approach to Training Volume; More Training, More Gaining. Stronger by Science. (Online article series)

**Key Findings:**
- 10-20 sets per muscle group per week is the general optimal range
- Beginners: 6-8 sets per muscle group per week sufficient
- Advanced: 15-20+ sets may be needed for continued progress
- Higher volumes = more growth, but with diminishing returns past ~20 sets
- Volume should be distributed across 2-3 sessions per week per muscle group
- "High" volume (>4 sets per exercise) enhances strength; "moderate" offers no advantage over high
- Progressive volume overload across mesocycles is key

**Programming Recommendations:**
- Start a mesocycle at ~10 sets/muscle/week
- Build to ~15-20 sets/muscle/week over 4-6 weeks
- Deload (reduce to ~6-8 sets) for 1 week
- Repeat with slightly higher starting volume
- For strength: >4 sets per exercise per session
- For hypertrophy: distribute 10-20 sets across 2-3 weekly sessions

**App Comparison:**
- Our app prescribes per-session sets only (4 for hypertrophy, 5 for strength)
- No weekly total volume guidance
- No volume progression within or across mesocycles
- **GAP: All the same volume gaps identified in Sources 1 and 9 apply here**

## Source 12: Starting Strength — Novice Linear Progression (Rippetoe)

**Citation:** Rippetoe, M. *Starting Strength: Basic Barbell Training*, 3rd ed. The Aasgaard Company, 2011. (Program structure from startingstrength.com)

**Key Findings:**
- 3x/week full-body training (Mon/Wed/Fri)
- 3 sets of 5 reps for squat, bench, press; 1 set of 5 for deadlift
- Linear progression: add 5-10 lbs per session (10 lbs initial squat, 5 lbs ongoing)
- Women/older: smaller increments (2.5 lbs)
- Phase 1: Squat, Press/Bench, Deadlift every session
- Phase 2: Replace some deadlifts with power cleans
- Phase 3: Add chin-ups, reduce deadlift frequency
- No deload protocol prescribed — stall → reset weight 10% and build back

**Programming Recommendations:**
- Novices should use simple linear progression
- 3x5 is the primary working set scheme
- Progress by adding weight every session until stalling
- When stalling: reset 10%, rebuild (may take 2-3 resets before moving to intermediate programming)
- Deadlift needs less volume (1x5) due to systemic fatigue
- Full-body 3x/week is optimal for novices

**App Comparison:**
- Our app suggests hypertrophy phase for beginners — SS would say **strength phase** (3x5 heavy)
- No concept of linear progression (adding weight each session)
- No novice-specific programming track
- **GAP: App should offer a "novice" path with linear progression (3x5, add weight each session)**
- **GAP: App should differentiate programming recommendations by experience level much more granularly**
- **GAP: App needs stall/plateau detection and reset protocol**

## Source 13: Barbell Medicine — The Bridge & RPE Autoregulation (Feigenbaum/Baraki)

**Citation:** Feigenbaum, J. & Baraki, A. The Bridge Program; Autoregulation and RPE. Barbell Medicine, 2017-present. (barbellmedicine.com)

**Key Findings:**
- The Bridge: 8-week program bridging novice → intermediate
- 3x/week, RPE-based (not percentage-based)
- Competition lifts + near-variations (pause squat, rack pulls, deficit deadlift, close-grip bench)
- RPE progression: Working sets ramp up RPE 6 → 7 → 8 across sets within a session
- Week-to-week: if RPE 8 felt controlled, add 1-5% next week
- Uses "WATER" model: Weight, Amount, Type, Effort, Regulation
- Key insight: "the external load is merely a strategy for eliciting a psychological and physiological response, which is unique to each trainee"
- Novices need ~2 weeks to learn accurate RPE ratings

**Programming Recommendations:**
- Transition from percentage to RPE when novice gains stall
- Use RPE to manage daily readiness variation
- Include exercise variations alongside main lifts
- Sessions: 70-90 minutes
- Program exercise type diversity: competition lifts, variations, and GPP

**App Comparison:**
- Our app uses RPE as fallback when 1RM not available — Barbell Medicine makes it primary
- No exercise variation recommendations
- No concept of the novice → intermediate transition
- **GAP: App should offer RPE-primary programming path for intermediate+ lifters**
- **GAP: App needs exercise variation library tied to each main lift**
- **GAP: App should detect when novice linear progression has stalled and suggest transitioning to intermediate programming**

# COMPREHENSIVE GAP ANALYSIS

## What the Evidence Recommends vs. What Our App Implements

### CRITICAL GAPS (High Impact, Evidence is Strong)

#### 1. Weekly Volume Programming (NOT Per-Session)
**Evidence says:** 10-20 sets per muscle group per week, distributed across 2-3 sessions
**App does:** Prescribes single-session sets only (4 hypertrophy, 5 strength, 4 peaking, 3 deload)
**Fix:** Add weekly volume targets and session distribution guidance

#### 2. Training Frequency Recommendations
**Evidence says:** Each muscle group 2x/week minimum; distribute volume across sessions
**App does:** Zero frequency guidance
**Fix:** Recommend frequency per lift per experience level (e.g., squat 2-3x, bench 3-4x, deadlift 1-2x)

#### 3. Volume Progression Within Mesocycles
**Evidence says:** Start at MEV, progress toward MAV over 4-6 weeks, then deload
**App does:** Static set prescriptions with no progression model
**Fix:** Implement 4-6 week mesocycle with weekly volume increases (e.g., Week 1: 3 sets → Week 4: 5 sets per exercise)

#### 4. Multi-Week Peaking/Taper Protocol
**Evidence says:** 1-2 week taper with 30-50% volume reduction, maintain intensity, 2-4 day cessation
**App does:** Single "peaking phase" prescription with no timeline
**Fix:** Implement meet prep timeline: 8 weeks out → 4 weeks out → 2 weeks out → 1 week → meet day

#### 5. Experience-Level-Specific Programming
**Evidence says:** Novices = linear progression (add weight each session); Intermediates = weekly/biweekly progression with RPE; Advanced = block periodization
**App does:** Same templates for everyone, only distinguishes by phase
**Fix:** Create distinct programming tracks: Novice (LP), Intermediate (DUP/RPE), Advanced (block periodization)

### IMPORTANT GAPS (Moderate Impact)

#### 6. Dual RPE + Percentage Prescriptions
**Evidence says:** Combine percentage targets with RPE check ("85% 1RM, should feel like RPE 8")
**App does:** Either percentage OR RPE, never both simultaneously
**Fix:** Display both when 1RM is known

#### 7. Autoregulated Volume (RPE Stops)
**Evidence says:** Perform sets until target RPE reached, rather than fixed set count
**App does:** Fixed set counts per phase
**Fix:** Add autoregulated option: "Perform sets at X weight until reaching RPE 8"

#### 8. Exercise Variation Recommendations
**Evidence says:** Include competition lifts + near-variations (pause squat, tempo deadlift, close-grip bench)
**App does:** Analyzes 6 exercises but doesn't recommend variations for each phase
**Fix:** Recommend exercise variants per phase (hypertrophy: tempo/paused variants; strength: competition lifts; peaking: competition lifts only)

#### 9. Mesocycle Structure
**Evidence says:** 4-6 week mesocycles with progressive overload, ending in deload
**App does:** Suggests phases but no mesocycle timeline
**Fix:** Implement mesocycle planner: "You're in Week 3 of 4 of your strength block"

#### 10. Back-Off Sets
**Evidence says:** After top working sets, perform 2-3 back-off sets at -10% for additional volume
**App does:** No back-off set concept
**Fix:** Add back-off set recommendations for strength and peaking phases

### NICE-TO-HAVE GAPS (Lower Priority)

#### 11. Per-Muscle-Group Volume Tracking
**Evidence says:** Track sets per muscle group per week against MEV/MAV/MRV landmarks
**App does:** No muscle group volume tracking
**Fix:** Optional advanced feature to track weekly set volume

#### 12. Plateau Detection and Reset Protocol
**Evidence says:** When progress stalls, reset load 10% and rebuild (SS); or switch variation (BBM)
**App does:** Suggests deload based on RPE/score trends, but no specific reset protocol
**Fix:** Detect plateau patterns and prescribe specific reset strategies

#### 13. Daily Undulating Periodization
**Evidence says:** Varying intensity daily (heavy day / light day / moderate day) is effective
**App does:** Single intensity range per phase
**Fix:** Offer DUP option with day-type recommendations

#### 14. Training-to-Failure Guidelines
**Evidence says:** Limit failure on heavy compounds; OK on lighter isolation work
**App does:** No guidance on training to failure
**Fix:** Add per-exercise proximity-to-failure recommendations

#### 15. Load Recommendations for Accessory Work
**Evidence says:** Accessories can use lighter loads (50-70%) for hypertrophy
**App does:** Same load recommendation regardless of exercise role
**Fix:** Differentiate main lift vs accessory load recommendations

## WHAT THE APP GETS RIGHT (Evidence-Aligned)

1. **Phase-based training model** (hypertrophy → strength → peaking → deload) — well-supported by periodization literature
2. **Intensity ranges per phase** — closely match evidence (65-75% hyp, 80-90% str, 90-100% peak)
3. **RPE estimation from movement quality** — novel and useful, with appropriate disclaimers
4. **Deload phase** (50-60% 1RM, RPE 5-6) — aligned with evidence on recovery weeks
5. **Rest period recommendations** (1-2 min hyp, 3-5 min str, 5+ peak) — evidence-supported
6. **Tempo guidance** (2-3s eccentric for hypertrophy) — matches Helms et al. recommendations
7. **Competition features** (depth judging, lockout, IPF rules, attempt planning) — well-implemented
8. **1RM estimation** (Epley + Brzycki) — standard validated formulas
9. **DOTS scoring** — correct IPF formula
10. **Phase suggestion algorithm** (score + RPE trends → next phase) — reasonable heuristic, though simplistic
11. **Fatigue detection** (first 2 vs last 2 rep score drop >15 points) — reasonable proxy based on Gonzalez-Badillo velocity-based fatigue work
